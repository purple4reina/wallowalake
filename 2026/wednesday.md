Then composers learned to paint with sound.

## Key Ideas

- **Impressionism** began with French painters like Monet — capture a *mood* or "impression," and the changing quality of *light*, instead of a photographic likeness.
- Monet painted the same subject over and over — *Haystacks*, *Rouen Cathedral*, *Water Lilies* — at different times of day and seasons. The haystack isn't really the subject; **the light is**.
- In music, Impressionism uses **tone color (timbre)** as the main expressive tool, built from orchestration, harmony, and texture.
- Harmony doesn't always "resolve" — dissonance is used for *color*, not drama.
- Simple or freely-flowing structures replace the strict forms of Classical music.
- The painter **Whistler** named his pictures with music words (*Nocturne*, *Symphony*, *Harmony*) to say they were about color and mood. **Debussy** borrowed the title *Nocturnes* back for music.
- Key composers: **Claude Debussy** (1862–1918) and **Maurice Ravel** (1875–1937), both from France.

<div class="info-box">
  <strong>Ravel's Boléro challenge:</strong> Impressionism usually avoids strong melody — so how is Boléro impressionist?
  It has almost <em>nothing but</em> melody. Listen for the answer: the entire interest is in the ever-changing
  orchestration and the slow swell of dynamics. That <em>is</em> impressionism — color over structure.
</div>
