From palace ballrooms to concert halls — how one dance form became one of the most
important structures in classical music, and how composers kept reinventing it for centuries.

## Key Ideas

- A Baroque **suite** is a collection of dances written for *listening*, not for dancing.
- The **Minuet** was the most fashionable dance in 1700s France. It has a strict **ABA form**: Minuet (A) → Trio (B) → Minuet again (A).
- The **Trio** (the B section) is lighter and quieter, often using fewer instruments.
- Beethoven swapped the polite Minuet for the **Scherzo** ("joke") — same ABA shape, still in three beats, but *much* faster and wilder.
- Composers kept using this dance form for centuries and stretched it in surprising ways — Tchaikovsky writes a waltz in **5/4** that always feels like it's stumbling; Schoenberg writes a Minuet with **no key at all**.
- Question to think about: *How does a composer know when a piece is done?*
