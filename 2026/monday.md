When I say "music history," what do you picture? This week is really music *appreciation* — learning to hear *more* in the music, not memorizing dates. Today we build the vocabulary you'll use all week to describe what you hear.

## Key Ideas

- Use **describing words**, not **feeling words**. "Loud," "fast," and "two melodies at once" are things we can all agree on by listening. "Exciting" or "sad" are opinions.
- **Timbre** is the quality that lets you tell a clarinet from a trumpet playing the same note. Every instrument belongs to a family: strings, winds, percussion, voice, or electronic.
- **Pitch** has a center: sing "do–re–mi…" and stop on "ti" — feel how badly it wants to land on "do"? That pull is the **tonic**. Some music removes it on purpose.
- **Texture** comes in layers: one voice alone is *monophony*, a melody with accompaniment is *homophony*, and several independent melodies at once is *polyphony* (like a round).
- Music history has five main **periods**: Medieval/Renaissance → Baroque → Classical → Romantic → Modern.
- A great listener stays curious, makes guesses, and is willing to be wrong.
