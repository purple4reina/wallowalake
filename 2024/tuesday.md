The Baroque era gave us one of the most sophisticated musical forms ever invented:
the fugue — a piece built entirely from one short melody, heard in multiple voices at once.

## Key Ideas

- **Baroque** (roughly 1600–1750): steady rhythms, mostly constant dynamics (no crescendos!), highly ornate melodies.
- **J.S. Bach** (1685–1750): Leipzig organist, music director of all Lutheran churches in Leipzig, father of 20 children (many also musicians). Known as the master of counterpoint.
- A **Fugue** is built from one idea — the **subject** — presented in multiple voices, one at a time:
  - **Subject** — the main melody
  - **Answer** — subject in a second voice while the first keeps going (counter-subject)
  - **Episode** — development passages between subject entries
  - **Stretto** — subjects overlapping, piling up with urgency
  - **Exposition** — the opening section where each voice enters for the first time
- A fugue stays in one mood — constant rhythm, tempo, texture (always polyphonic), dynamics.
- Modern composers still write fugues — they don't belong to any one era.
