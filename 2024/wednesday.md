One invention changed everything: the crescendo.
The Classical era brought the Enlightenment's ideals — clarity, balance, and the individual — into the concert hall.

## Key Ideas

- **Classical** (roughly 1750–1820): coincides with the American and French Revolutions — an era that valued reason, the individual, and democratic ideals.
- The **Mannheim school** (active ~1740–1778) popularized two game-changing effects:
  - **Mannheim Rocket** — a fast, rising arpeggiated melody with a crescendo
  - **Mannheim Roller** (also "steamroller") — a long, extended crescendo over a rising melody and steady bass
- **Mozart** (1756–1791): child prodigy from Salzburg, died at 36. Masterworks in piano, opera, chamber music, and symphony.
- The **crescendo** was brand new in the Classical era — Baroque music had no gradual dynamic changes, only sudden shifts.

<div class="info-box">
  <strong>Spot the difference:</strong> Baroque = sudden loud/soft (terraced dynamics). Classical = gradual crescendo/diminuendo. This is one of the clearest ways to tell them apart just by listening.
</div>
