The Romantic era took everything Classical and made it *more* —
bigger orchestras, wider dynamics, more complex harmonies, deeper emotions.
And running through it all: one ancient melody.

## Key Ideas

- **Romantic** (roughly 1820–1900): larger orchestras, extreme dynamic range, chromatic harmony, tone poems that tell stories.
- The same Classical forms (sonata, symphony, concerto) continue — but stretched to new extremes.
- **Tone poems** — orchestral works with no strict form, instead painting a picture or telling a story in music.
- **Dies Irae** ("Day of Wrath") — a Gregorian chant melody from the medieval funeral mass, written between the 600s and 1200s. Romantic composers quoted it constantly to evoke death, horror, and the supernatural. So did 20th-century film composers.
