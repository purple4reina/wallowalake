One story, four operas, sixteen hours of music.
Wagner spent 26 years writing *Der Ring des Nibelungen* — and it changed what opera could be forever.

## Key Ideas

- **Richard Wagner** (1813–1883): German composer, best known for his operas. Moved music from the Romantic Period into Modernity. (Also: a deeply unpleasant person whose anti-Semitic writings caused real harm — that's part of his history too.)
- **Before Wagner**: opera had clear sections — sung dialog (*recitative*) and standalone songs (*arias*) — with the orchestra in a supporting role.
- **Wagner's genius**: *through-composed* opera — no distinction between dialog and aria. The orchestra drives the drama as much as the singers do.
- **Gesamtkunstwerk** ("total artwork"): Wagner's idea that opera should unify music, poetry, visual art, and theater into a single seamless experience. He designed and built his own opera house in **Bayreuth** specifically to realize this vision.
- **Leitmotivs**: short musical ideas — a few notes or a short phrase — that represent a specific character, object, or idea. They appear mainly in the orchestra, not sung. Once you know them, they reveal a hidden layer of meaning in the story.

## The Ring: Plot Summary

Four operas based on Norse/Germanic mythology — a story of gods, giants, dwarves, a cursed ring, and the end of the world:

1. **Das Rheingold** — The dwarf Alberich steals gold from the Rhine and forges a ring of ultimate power. The god Wotan takes it, triggering a curse.
2. **Die Walküre** — Wotan's twin children, Siegmund and Sieglinde, are reunited. His daughter Brünnhilde defies him and is punished: put to sleep, surrounded by fire.
3. **Siegfried** — Sieglinde's son Siegfried reforges the broken sword, kills the dragon Fafner, and wakes Brünnhilde with a kiss.
4. **Götterdämmerung** (Twilight of the Gods) — Siegfried is betrayed and murdered. Brünnhilde rides into his funeral pyre. The gods' hall burns. The ring returns to the Rhine.

*You'll recognize pieces of this story: a single ring that grants ultimate power (Tolkien), twins separated at birth (Star Wars), a sword only a hero can claim (Arthurian legend), a sleeping woman woken by a kiss (Sleeping Beauty), a dragon guarding gold…*

## Key Leitmotivs

| Leitmotiv | What it represents |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Valhalla | The gods' hall — majestic, stable |
| The Giants | Heavy, plodding — low brass |
| The Sword | Bright, heroic — rising line |
| The Valkyries | Fierce, rhythmic — the war-maidens |
| The Dragon | Dark, chromatic |
| Heroic Siegfried | Bold, expansive |
| Siegfried's Horn Call | Youthful, confident |
| The Woodbird | Light, high, fluttering |
