Beethoven straddled two eras — Classical in form, Romantic in feeling.
And the structure he mastered, **Sonata-Allegro Form**, is still one of the most important in all of music.

## Key Ideas

- **Sonata-Allegro Form** developed in the Classical Period (1750–1800) and continued well beyond it. It's the structure of most first movements of symphonies, sonatas, and concertos.

  | Section | What happens |
  |---------|-------------|
  | **Exposition** | Theme A (home key) → Theme B (new key) — often repeated |
  | **Development** | Both themes get pulled apart, transformed, pushed through distant keys |
  | **Recapitulation** | Theme A and Theme B return — both now in the home key |

- **Ludwig van Beethoven** (1770–1827): born in Bonn, moved to Vienna. Planned to study with Mozart but arrived too late — Mozart had died. Studied with Haydn instead.
- Best known for his symphonies, piano sonatas, and string quartets.
- His **deafness** began in his mid-20s and became total. He kept composing entirely from musical imagination.
- His work bridges the Classical and Romantic eras — Classical in structure, but emotionally vast in a way that was brand new.

<div class="info-box">
  <strong>From his diary, 1802 (Heiligenstädter Testament):</strong><br>
  <em>"O you men who think or say that I am malevolent, stubborn or misanthropic, how greatly do you wrong me… for six years I have been a hopeless case… born with an ardent and lively temperament… I was compelled early to isolate myself, to live in loneliness…"</em>
  <br><br>
  At the premiere of his 9th Symphony, Beethoven was still conducting when the audience erupted in applause — he couldn't hear it. A singer had to walk over and turn him around to face the crowd.
</div>
