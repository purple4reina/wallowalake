For 300 years, composers used the same handful of forms. Then the 20th century blew them up.

## Key Ideas

- After centuries of sonata-allegro, rondo, and minuet, 20th-century composers felt an urgent need for something new — often shaped by the world events around them.
- **Tone poems** — no strict form; instead, music tells a story or paints a picture. Common from the late Romantic era into the 20th century.
- **Serialism (12-tone)** — a democratic system where every pitch gets equal use; no home key. Associated with composers who consciously rejected the music that had been celebrated by the Nazis.
- **Minimalism** — born in 1960s America as a reaction against the complexity and dissonance of serialism. Very short musical ideas repeated over and over, slowly evolving.

    </a>
    <span class="rec-note">Impressionism: the goal is mood and atmosphere, not a clear melody or narrative. Notice how the harmony never quite resolves — it floats.</span>

</ul>
